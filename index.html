What does this visa application need?

Pick a destination, purpose and situation, and the checklist rebuilds itself from a rules file. This is the core idea of the whole system, running in your browser.

    Sample rules for learning only; real requirements vary by embassy and applicant. Always confirm requirements on the embassy or visa center website. Your choices are saved only in this browser.

    Is my passport valid enough?

    Enter your passport expiry date and the date you plan to return. The checker tells you whether the passport lasts long enough.

    Rules differ by country, so confirm the exact one on the embassy website. Schengen also asks that the passport was issued within the last 10 years.

    Estimate the total cost

    Enter each amount from the official fee pages and from your own price list. Nothing is pre-filled, because fees change.

    Amounts are per applicant. The currency only changes how totals are shown, so enter every amount in the same currency. Visa fees are usually not refunded if the visa is refused.

    Plan your timeline

    Enter your travel date and adjust the waiting times for your case. The planner counts backward and shows when to start.

    The waiting times are starting values for planning, not promises. Check the visa center for current appointment waits and the embassy for processing times. Some centers accept applications only up to a set time before travel, often 6 months.

    Am I ready to apply?

    Tick what is true for you. The quiz shows what is still missing and where to find help. It is only a self-check: the embassy decides, and nothing here predicts the result.

    Build your itinerary

    Add your dates and your plan for each day, then print the itinerary to attach to your application. Plan only what you really intend to do, and match the dates to your application and your bookings.

    How travel platforms work

    A travel platform rarely owns anything. It connects travelers to suppliers and handles search, booking, payment and support around them.

    LayerWhat it does
    SupplyFlights from GDS systems (Amadeus, Sabre, Travelport) and airline APIs; hotels from channel managers and bedbanks; cars, buses and tours from their own APIs.
    SearchCalls many suppliers at once, normalizes the replies, removes duplicates, ranks results. Short-lived caching keeps it fast.
    BookingRe-check price, hold inventory, collect traveler details, take payment, confirm with the supplier, issue the ticket or voucher.
    PaymentsMulti-currency pricing, cards plus local methods such as mobile money, fraud checks, supplier settlement.
    After bookingChanges, cancellations, refunds, notifications, support.

    The hardest case is when payment succeeds but the supplier rejects the booking. The system must refund or rebook automatically.

    Systems that support visa applications

    • Official portals: e-visa and application sites that enforce required fields.
    • Visa application centers: companies such as VFS Global and TLScontact that collect files, biometrics and fees.
    • Appointment systems: slots, confirmations and reminders.
    • Document management: passports, statements and letters stored with status and expiry dates, like stock items in inventory software.
    • Checklist engines: a rules database that lists the documents needed for each case.
    • Form and validation tools: auto-fill from a stored profile and flag mismatches.
    • Supporting documents: flight reservations, hotel confirmations, travel insurance.
    • Fees and case tracking: payments with receipts, and a CRM that follows each file to the decision.
    • Translation and legalization: certified translation and notarization.

    Application journey

    Every application follows the same path, from the first question to the returned passport.

    1. AskThe client says where they want to go and why. The system asks a few questions: purpose, dates, job, who pays.
    2. Get the checklistThe rules engine builds the personal list of documents, with the reason for each.
    3. Collect documentsThe client uploads photos or scans, or an agent does it for them. Missing items stay flagged.
    4. CheckAutomatic checks catch expired passports, wrong sizes and names that do not match. An agent approves or rejects each document with a reason.
    5. Fill the formThe application form is filled from the client’s profile and compared with the passport.
    6. Pay and bookThe client pays the visa and service fees and books the appointment at the visa center or embassy.
    7. SubmitThe client attends with the final package. Biometrics are taken if required.
    8. TrackThe case is followed during processing, with updates by WhatsApp, SMS or email.
    9. Decision and passport returnedGranted or refused, the client collects the passport. After a refusal, the agent goes through the reasons with them.

    Refusal reasons

    These are common reasons for a refusal or a delay. Each one can be prevented.

    ReasonWhy it happensWhat to do
    Unclear or insufficient fundsBank statements show a low balance or sudden large deposits, or do not cover the trip.Show steady income and savings over 6 months, explain large deposits with documents, and add a sponsor’s papers if someone else pays.
    Names or details that do not matchSpelling or dates differ between the passport, form, tickets and letters.Copy the passport exactly everywhere and compare every document with it before submitting.
    Weak ties to the home countryThe officer is not convinced that the applicant will return.Include proof of a job, family, property or studies, and a clear plan for the return.
    Unclear purpose or itineraryThe reason for the trip or the plan seems inconsistent or contradicts other documents.Give a short, consistent explanation of the trip and an itinerary that matches the bookings and dates.
    Missing or incomplete documentsA required paper is missing, expired, unreadable or in the wrong format.Use the checklist, tick every item and have an agent review the file before the appointment.
    Problems with previous travelEarlier overstays, refusals that were not declared, or unexplained gaps in travel history.Declare past refusals honestly and explain them. Do not hide anything.
    Doubts about authenticityA document looks altered or cannot be verified.Submit only genuine documents. Get official copies, with certified translation or legalization when required.
    Insurance or booking problemsThe insurance does not meet the minimum, or the reservations do not cover the travel dates.Check the minimum cover and make the dates match across insurance, flight and hotel.

    Reasons vary by embassy. If a visa is refused, read the refusal letter, fix the reasons it gives and apply again with new evidence, rather than resubmitting the same file.

    Bank statement guide

    Embassies use bank statements to check three things: that you can pay for the trip, that your money comes from a normal source, and that you have reasons to go home. This guide is general, because each embassy sets its own rules. It is based on public guides and was checked in November 2026.

    What officers look for

    • Steady income: a salary or business income that arrives regularly.
    • A balance that is stable over time, not a sudden jump just before you apply.
    • Enough money for the whole trip, including tickets, accommodation and daily costs.
    • Normal spending that matches your job and lifestyle.
    • Your name and account details matching your passport and other documents.

    How many months

    Many guides say about 3 months for Schengen and about 6 months for the UK, Canada, Australia and the US, but the embassy’s own list always wins. The UK student route has its own rule: the money must stay in the account for at least 28 consecutive days.

    Large deposits

    A large deposit just before you apply can look like borrowed money. If the money is genuine, explain it with proof, for example a property sale contract, an employer’s bonus letter, a business invoice or a transfer from your own savings account. Add a short letter that explains the source.

    Before you print your statements

    • Use the account where your income really goes.
    • Include every page, readable, with the bank’s name and your account details.
    • Ask the bank to stamp or sign the statements if the embassy requires it.
    • Do not move money in just for the visa, and do not borrow funds to reach a target.
    • Never submit altered or fake statements. This can lead to a refusal and a ban.

    In Cameroon

    Ask your bank how long it takes to produce stamped statements and plan for it in your timeline. Mobile money records can support your story, but check whether the centre accepts them as proof of funds.

    Travel insurance guide

    Travel medical insurance is a required document for a Schengen visa and a strong safeguard on other trips. This guide is based on consular pages and public guides, and was checked in November 2026.

    Schengen visa: what the policy must cover

    • Valid in all Schengen states, not only the country you will visit first.
    • Valid for the whole stay, with dates that match your application and your bookings.
    • At least €30,000 of cover for medical costs.
    • Includes repatriation for medical reasons or death.
    • Includes urgent medical care and emergency hospital treatment.

    Check the certificate before you submit

    • Your name matches the passport exactly.
    • The start and end dates match your visa application and your flight and hotel bookings.
    • The text mentions the Schengen area, the €30,000 amount, repatriation and emergency hospital treatment.
    • It is a complete certificate with a policy number, not a screenshot. Some consulates want a specific language, so check.
    • Ask whether the policy is refunded if the visa is refused.

    Other destinations

    For visitor visas to the UK, Canada, the US and Australia, insurance is not normally a visa requirement, but treatment abroad can be very expensive, so it is strongly recommended. Check each embassy’s current list. For an Australian student visa, Overseas Student Health Cover is compulsory.

    In Cameroon

    Some consulates ask for a letter from the insurer confirming that the policy meets the rules. Domestic plans rarely meet Schengen wording, so choose an international travel medical policy. Before you pay, ask the consulate or visa centre what it accepts, and keep the receipt.

    Interview preparation

    Interviews matter most for the US visa, and they can happen for other visas too. They are short, so clear and honest answers count. This guide helps clients prepare, not memorize a script. It is based on public guides and was checked in November 2026.

    What to expect

    • A short conversation, often only a few minutes.
    • Your identity and the details of your DS-160 are checked.
    • Questions about your trip, your job, how you pay and your ties to Cameroon.
    • The officer usually decides at the end.

    Topics to prepare

    • The purpose and length of your trip.
    • Where you will stay and what you will do.
    • Your job, your salary and how long you have worked there.
    • Who pays for the trip.
    • Relatives or friends in the destination country.
    • Earlier travel and any previous refusal.
    • What brings you back to Cameroon: job, home, family or studies.

    How to answer

    • Answer the question you are asked, in a few clear sentences.
    • Be honest and consistent with your documents. Never invent a reason, a job or a sponsor.
    • Know your own file: dates, amounts, names and addresses.
    • Bring your documents, even if the officer does not ask for them.
    • If you do not understand a question, ask the officer to repeat it.

    Why refusals happen

    The most common reason for a US refusal is Section 214(b): the officer was not convinced that the trip is temporary or that you will return. Ties are the parts of your life that bind you to your home country, such as a job, a home and your family.

    If you are refused

    Ask for the reason, read any paper you are given, and fix the real problem before you apply again. A new application with the same file may lead to the same answer.

    Photo requirements

    A photo that does not follow the rules can delay an application. Use this list before the studio visit.

    Usually accepted

    • Taken in the last 6 months
    • Plain white or light background, no shadows
    • Face fully visible, looking straight at the camera
    • Neutral expression with the mouth closed
    • Sharp, well lit and in good print or file quality

    Often rejected

    • An old photo, or one that no longer looks like you
    • Filters, retouching or a photo of a photo
    • Shadows, glare on glasses or a patterned background
    • Head or face covered, except for religious or medical reasons with the face still visible
    • Wrong size, or cropped too close or too far

    Usual sizes

    DestinationUsual size
    Schengen area35 × 45 mm
    United States2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm), usually uploaded as a digital file

    Sizes are typical. Check the embassy or visa center for the exact size, file format and file size for your application.

    Glossary

    Plain-language meanings of the terms used in this guide.

    GDS
    Global distribution system. A network that gathers airline, hotel and car inventory so agents can search and book it. Examples: Amadeus, Sabre, Travelport.
    NDC
    New Distribution Capability. A standard that lets airlines sell fares and extras directly to agents and platforms.
    Bedbank
    A wholesaler that buys hotel rooms in bulk and resells them to travel platforms at net rates.
    Channel manager
    Software that lets a hotel update prices and availability on many booking sites at once.
    Aggregator
    A service that connects many suppliers through a single API.
    Commission
    A percentage a supplier pays the platform for each booking it sells.
    Markup
    An amount a platform adds on top of the supplier’s price.
    Biometrics
    Fingerprints and a photo taken at a visa center to identify the applicant.
    Visa application center
    A company authorized by an embassy to receive applications, check documents and take biometrics. It does not decide whether the visa is granted.
    E-visa
    A visa applied for and issued online.
    Legalization
    Official confirmation that a document is genuine so another country accepts it. Depending on the country, it may be called an apostille or authentication.
    Notarization
    A notary certifies that a copy or a signature is genuine.
    Certified translation
    A translation signed by an approved translator who states that it is accurate.
    Proof of ties
    Evidence that you will return home, such as a job, family, property or studies.
    Sponsor
    A person or organization that pays for the trip or vouches for the applicant.
    Rules engine
    The part of the system that picks the documents required for each case.
    Audit trail
    A record of who changed what, and when.

    United States: points to check before paying

    Media reports say Cameroon is on the list of countries where immigrant visa issuance has been paused since 21 January 2026. The same reports say the pause does not cover tourist or business visas. The US also applies visa suspensions and, for some applicants, visa bonds of several thousand dollars. Check the State Department website for the current lists before a client pays any fee.

    Booking safely

    Book appointments only on the official center website. Anyone who sells appointments, or asks for payment by phone, may be committing fraud.

    Paying fees

    MTN Mobile Money and Orange Money cover most everyday payments in Cameroon, and the state treasury accepts them for public fees. Visa centers are different. Each center sets its own payment methods, and the French centers take fees on site, usually by bank card or other locally accepted methods. Do not promise a client that mobile money works at a visa center. Check the center’s payment page first. A platform can still offer mobile money for its own service fees.

    Legalization and translation

    According to the guides we found, Cameroon has not joined the Apostille Convention. Cameroonian documents therefore normally need authentication by the Ministry of External Relations (MINREX), then legalization by the destination country’s embassy. For Belgium, the MINREX authentication must not be older than 3 months. Documents in another language usually need a sworn translation. MINREX also runs an online platform called LEGALIS for authenticating official documents.

    Only the routes above were checked, and some details come from news reports. Rules change often, so confirm each one on the official site before advising a client.

    Study in France from Cameroon

    Students from Cameroon must complete the Campus France procedure before they can apply for a French student visa. Checked in November 2026 from public pages, so confirm every step on the official sites.

    1. Create your fileRegister on the Études en France platform run by Campus France, fill in your file and choose your programs. Start about a year before the start of your studies.
    2. Pay the file feeIf it is requested, pay the Campus France file fee. Reported amounts differ between websites, so check the current amount with Campus France Cameroon.
    3. Attend the interviewCampus France Cameroon holds the interview, where an adviser checks that your study plan is clear and consistent. It has offices in Yaoundé and Douala.
    4. Get your Campus France certificateOnce the procedure is complete, you receive the Campus France certificate that you need for the visa file. Keep your admission or pre-enrolment letter with it.
    5. Book at TLScontactSince 2 May 2024, students must finish the Campus France procedure before booking their visa appointment at TLScontact.
    6. File the long-stay visa applicationApply for the long-stay student visa (VLS-TS) with the application form, your passport and photos, the admission letter, the Campus France certificate and proof that you can pay for your stay.
    7. Validate your visa in FranceOn arrival, validate the visa with the French immigration services (ANEF, formerly OFII).

    A reported rule for private schools

    A Cameroonian news site reports that since 9 July 2026, Campus France Cameroon asks candidates admitted to private higher-education schools in France to show full payment of one year’s tuition, or proof that the amount is available in a blocked account, when they file at TLScontact. Public universities were reported as not affected. This comes from a news site, not an official page, so confirm it with Campus France Cameroon before you pay any school.

    Before you pay a school

    Ask the school in writing what happens to your payment if the visa is refused. Refunds depend on the school, and Campus France says refunds are not its responsibility.

    Other study destinations have their own procedures. Check the embassy page of the country you are applying to.

    US visitor visa from Cameroon

    A short guide to applying for a US visitor visa (B-1/B-2) from Cameroon. Checked in November 2026 from public pages. US rules change often, so confirm every step on the embassy and State Department sites.

    1. Complete the DS-160Fill in the DS-160 online application on the State Department’s CEAC site. The form itself is free. Print the confirmation page with the barcode.
    2. Create your account and payCreate a profile on the US visa appointment website for Cameroon and pay the application fee. Check the State Department website for the current amount.
    3. Book your interview in YaoundéSchedule the interview at the US Embassy in Yaoundé. Since July 2025 every nonimmigrant visa applicant in Cameroon must attend in person, and the interview waiver is suspended. Book early, because waiting times vary.
    4. Prepare your documentsBring your passport, the DS-160 confirmation page, the appointment letter, the fee receipt and a photo. Add evidence of your trip purpose, your funds and your ties to Cameroon, such as a job, family, property or studies.
    5. Attend the interviewAnswer honestly and briefly. The consular officer usually decides at the interview. If the visa is approved, an issuance fee is paid; the embassy announced $35 in July 2025.
    6. After approvalIn July 2025 the embassy said most visas for Cameroonians are issued as single-entry and valid for 3 months. A visa lets you travel to the US, but border officers decide how long you may stay.

    Good to know

    Passport holders from Cameroon generally need a transit visa even to change planes in the US, and Cameroon is not part of the visa waiver program or ESTA.

    Before a client pays any fee, read the United States notes in the Applying from Cameroon section: the reported immigrant visa pause, visa suspensions and visa bonds.

    Canada visa from Cameroon

    A short guide to applying for a Canadian visitor visa or study permit from Cameroon. Checked in November 2026 from public pages. Canadian rules changed a lot between 2024 and 2026, so confirm every step on the IRCC website.

    Visitor visa

    1. Gather your documentsPrepare a valid passport, proof of funds, proof of ties to Cameroon (a job, family, property or studies), a clear purpose for the trip, and an invitation letter if you are visiting family or friends.
    2. Apply onlineCreate an IRCC secure account, fill in the form, upload your documents and pay the fees. Each traveler, including children, files a separate application.
    3. Receive your biometrics letterAfter you pay, IRCC sends a biometrics instruction letter in your account. You normally have 30 days to give your biometrics.
    4. Give biometrics in YaoundéBook an appointment at the Canada Visa Application Centre in Yaoundé and bring the instruction letter and your passport.
    5. Wait for the decisionFollow your IRCC account and answer any request for more information quickly. Apply early, because processing times vary, and avoid non-refundable bookings until you have your visa.

    Study permit

    1. Get a letter of acceptanceApply to a designated learning institution (DLI) and get its letter of acceptance. Check that the school is on IRCC’s DLI list.
    2. Get your attestation letterMost applicants need a provincial or territorial attestation letter (PAL/TAL), usually obtained through the school. Students going to Quebec need a Quebec Acceptance Certificate (CAQ) from the Quebec government first.
    3. Prove your fundsShow that you can pay your first-year tuition plus the living costs that IRCC requires. The required amount has increased, so check the current figure on the IRCC website.
    4. Apply onlineApply through your IRCC secure account with the acceptance letter, the attestation letter or CAQ, proof of funds, your passport, photos and a study plan. Paper applications are only accepted in special cases.
    5. Give biometricsIf IRCC asks for biometrics, give them at the visa application centre within the time shown in your instruction letter.
    6. Medical exam and police certificateDo these only if IRCC asks for them for your country or program, and follow your account for updates.

    Good to know

    Canada does not issue visitor visas on arrival, so you must have the visa before you travel.

    Study permit rules changed several times between 2024 and 2026, with national caps, attestation letters and higher funds. Use only current guides.

    For the Yaoundé centre, see the Applying from Cameroon section.

    UK visa from Cameroon

    A short guide to the UK Standard Visitor visa for applicants from Cameroon, with an important warning about UK student visas. Checked in November 2026 from public pages, so confirm every step on GOV.UK.

    Important: student visas

    Since 26 March 2026, the UK refuses new Student visa applications made from outside the UK by nationals of Cameroon, Afghanistan, Myanmar and Sudan. This is called the visa brake. It does not cancel visas already granted or affect people already in the UK. The High Court dismissed legal challenges to it in 2026, and one report says it will last at least 18 months. Do not pay a UK school deposit or a Student visa fee before checking GOV.UK.

    The Home Office says the brake will be reviewed regularly and may be extended, changed or lifted. It covers Student visas, not Standard Visitor visas.

    Standard Visitor visa

    1. Gather your documentsPrepare a passport valid beyond your trip, bank statements or payslips covering 6 months, accommodation and travel details, and proof of ties to Cameroon. Add an invitation letter if you will stay with someone.
    2. Apply online on GOV.UKFill in the Standard Visitor visa application on GOV.UK and pay the fee. You can apply up to 3 months before your trip.
    3. Book your biometrics appointmentBook an appointment at the UK Visa Application Centre in Yaoundé, run by VFS Global, to give your fingerprints and photo.
    4. Attend and submitBring your passport, the appointment confirmation and your documents, and arrive early.
    5. Wait for the decisionDo not pay for flights until you have the visa. Check the visa details when you get your passport back.

    Good to know

    Sources disagree on whether the UK visa centre in Douala is open, so check VFS Global for the current centres.

    For the Yaoundé centre, see the Applying from Cameroon section.

    Australia visa from Cameroon

    A short guide to the Australian Visitor visa (subclass 600) and Student visa (subclass 500) for applicants from Cameroon. Checked in November 2026 from public pages, so confirm every step on the Home Affairs website. No Cameroon-specific ban was found, but student visas face very high scrutiny.

    Visitor visa (subclass 600)

    1. Choose your streamPick the stream that fits your trip: tourist, sponsored family, business visitor or frequent traveller. Apply online from outside Australia.
    2. Gather your documentsPrepare a valid passport, a photo, proof of funds, proof that your stay is temporary (a job, family, property or studies) and your travel plan. Add your host’s details if a relative in Australia invites you.
    3. Apply through ImmiAccountCreate an ImmiAccount, complete the application, upload your documents and pay. Upload everything when you apply, because missing documents can delay or sink the application.
    4. Health exam and biometrics if askedHome Affairs may ask for a health examination or biometrics, usually with a short deadline. We could not confirm a biometrics centre in Cameroon, so check the VFS Global pages for Australia.
    5. Wait before bookingDo not buy non-refundable tickets until you have the visa. Processing times vary by stream, from a few days to several weeks.

    Student visa (subclass 500)

    1. Get your Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE)Accept an offer from a registered Australian provider and get your CoE.
    2. Buy Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC)OSHC is compulsory for the whole length of the visa. You need the policy confirmation before you apply.
    3. Prepare your financial evidenceShow funds for tuition, living costs and travel with bank statements, a loan, a scholarship letter or a sponsor’s letter. The required amounts change, so check Home Affairs.
    4. Prepare your Genuine Student answersExplain why you want this course, why in Australia and how it fits your plans at home. Officers also consider whether similar courses are available near you.
    5. English test, police clearance and translationsAdd your English test result and a police clearance. Documents not in English need a translation by a NAATI-authorised translator.
    6. Apply, then follow upApply through ImmiAccount. If Home Affairs asks for biometrics or a health examination, do it quickly.

    Good to know

    Student visa refusals are at a ten-year high: the refusal rate reached 24.2 per cent in June 2026. Reports describe the application charge as high and non-refundable. Check the current fee on the Home Affairs website.

    In November 2026 the Australian government opened a consultation on reforms to the Genuine Student test and the financial requirements, so these rules may change soon.

    People with a Visitor visa generally cannot apply for a Student visa from inside Australia, so do not plan to switch visas after arriving.

      Checklist system design
      

      Data model
      

      

      TableHolds
      

      clientsName as in passport, nationality, birth date, passport number and expiry, occupation, contacts
      

      applicationsOne client, one destination, visa type, travel dates, purpose, status
      

      requirementsOne document an embassy asks for, with conditions, format rules and source link
      

      documentsThe stored file: type, issue and expiry dates, copies needed, original or copy
      

      application_documentsLinks a requirement to the document that satisfies it, with a status
      

      

      Rules as data
      

      Requirements change often, so keep them in a data file rather than in code. Each rule records its source and when it was last checked.
      

      {
  "id": "emp-letter",
  "name": "Employment letter and last 3 payslips",
  "when": { "job": "employed" },
  "source": "embassy page URL",
  "verified": "2026-11-01"
}
      

      Validation
      

        

      • Automatic: passport validity against travel dates, file type and size, photo dimensions, expired documents, name spelling across documents.
        • 

      • Cross-checks: hotel and flight dates match the application.
        • 

      • Human review: an agent approves or rejects each document with a reason.
        • 

      

      Workflow
      

      Each requirement moves through: not started, uploaded, under review, approved or rejected, submitted. The application moves through: collecting documents, ready for submission, appointment booked, submitted, decision, passport returned.
      

      Practical notes
      

        

      • Encrypt stored files, because passports are sensitive.
        • 

      • Allow phone-camera uploads and agent uploads on a client’s behalf.
        • 

      • Send reminders by WhatsApp and SMS as well as email.
        • 

      • Build a final package in the embassy’s required order, with an audit trail of who changed what.
        • 

      

      


      

      Keeping rules up to date
      

      Embassy requirements change without notice. A rule is only useful while someone keeps it current.
      

        

      1. Record the sourceEvery rule stores the embassy or visa center page it came from, and the date it was last checked.
        2. 

      2. Review on a scheduleCheck the most requested destinations every week and the others every month. Check again before a busy season.
        3. 

      3. Watch for changesSubscribe to embassy notices, follow the official pages and note changes that clients report after an appointment.
        4. 

      4. Update and testEdit the rule in the data file, try a few typical cases (for example employed, self-employed, minor) and have a second person check it.
        5. 

      5. Date and announceChange the verified date, note what changed and tell the agents so open applications can be corrected.
        6. 

      

      What each rule stores
      

      

      FieldExample
      

      idemp-letter
      

      nameEmployment letter and last 3 payslips
      

      whenThe conditions that make the rule apply, such as job = employed
      

      sourceA link to the embassy or visa center page
      

      verifiedThe date someone last checked the rule
      

      ownerThe team member responsible for keeping it current
      

      

      Handling old rules
      

      A rule that has not been checked for more than 60 days should show as “needs checking” to agents, and clients should see a reminder to confirm with the embassy.

      FAQ and contact
      

      Do you guarantee that my visa will be approved?
      No. Only the embassy decides whether a visa is granted. We help you prepare a complete, correct file.
      

      How long does a visa take?
      It depends on the destination, the season and your case. Use the timeline planner and adjust the waiting times for your center.
      

      Can I upload my documents here?
      No. This site is a guide and a demo. It does not collect or store passports or other documents.
      

      Is the checklist official?
      No. It uses sample rules for learning. Always confirm requirements with the embassy or visa center.
      

      Can I pay visa fees with mobile money?
      It depends on the center. Each one sets its own payment methods, so check its payment page before you plan.
      

      What if my visa is refused?
      Read the refusal letter, fix the reasons it gives and apply again with new evidence. See the refusal reasons section.
      

      Do I need to translate my documents?
      Often yes, with a certified or sworn translation, when a document is not in a language the embassy accepts. Ask the embassy which languages it takes.
      

      Do I need travel insurance?
      For a Schengen visa, yes. Other destinations differ, so check the checklist and the embassy page.
